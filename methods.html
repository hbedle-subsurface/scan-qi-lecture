Methods

Details of the data preparation, attributes, well logs and crossplots, self-organizing map (SOM) and SHAP values used in the SCAN029 case. The crossplots, the SOM and the SHAP values are computed in the browser.

1. Seismic data and well control

SCAN029 (L2EBN2020ASCAN029) is a 2D pre-stack time-migrated line acquired in 2020 for EBN and TNO. The full, near, mid and far angle stacks are read from 0 to 3.0 s along the whole line (CDP 100–19492, 2.5 m CDP interval). The data are zero phase, and an increase in acoustic impedance is recorded as a negative number.

The stacks have no gain applied after migration. One gain curve is applied to every trace: the inverse of the smoothed median RMS amplitude at each two-way time. Because the same curve is used on every trace, amplitude differences along the line are preserved.

Well tops are placed in two-way time with Dix interval velocities from the migration velocities at the velocity location nearest each point of the well path. None of the three wells has a sonic log or checkshot in the available data. CAL-GT-04 is projected with its deviation survey; the CAL-GT-01 path is estimated from its end-point offsets; ASTEN-GT-02 is vertical and 0.1 km from the line.

2. Attributes

Attributes are computed on every eighth CDP (20 m) and exported every 4 ms. Most are averaged over 140 m along the line and 62 ms in time, so that they describe the character of a seismic facies rather than individual reflections. Relative acoustic impedance and AVT are averaged over 140 m and 10 ms, the four stack amplitudes over 140 m only, and the three phase attributes are not averaged, because an average across the ±180° phase wrap has no physical meaning. Relative acoustic impedance, RMS amplitude and AVT were computed in AASPI; the other attributes were computed in Python. Each attribute's page in the Attributes stage gives what it measures and its original reference.

2.1 Distance and quadrant (DQ) attributes

Six DQ attributes are also available: DQ, ThetaPX, the signed isochron, the signed half isochron, and DQ averaged and summed over each half cycle. They were computed in AASPI from the near and far stacks, which were reversed to SEG normal polarity (an impedance increase positive) before the run, so their signs are the opposite of the seismic display here (Bedle et al., 2025a, 2025b; Choudhry et al., 2026a, 2026b). The files come from the scan-dq repository, where they are on the same 20 m traces at 2 ms from 0.15 to 2.0 s; here every second sample is kept, with no averaging along the line or in time. The DQ attributes therefore describe individual half cycles of the near stack, while most other attributes are averaged over 62 ms, and they have no values below 2.0 s, so they are left out of displays, crossplots and SOM windows there. A SOM that includes a DQ attribute is trained and classified down to 2.0 s. The StickOgram and quadrant number are steps of the workflow and not measurements of the rock, so they are not offered. The DQ workflow was developed and published on siliciclastic sections; the Chalk and the Dinantian carbonate on this line lie outside those settings. DQ attributes are shown on the color bars of the AASPI DQ attribute pamphlet, with unfilled wiggle traces of DQ at 2 ms drawn over the color. The DQ calculation is not part of this repository.

3. Well logs and crossplots

3.1 Logs

ASTEN-GT-02 has a composite of gamma ray, bulk density, density correction and neutron logs from the 1987 logging runs (TNO; depths below rotary table, 30.34 m above NAP), and a temperature log. Gamma ray covers 450–1661 m and density and neutron 553–1638 m, so the Houthem Formation, from 1635 m to total depth at 1673 m, has gamma ray over its upper 26 m and density over its upper 3 m. The neutron values in the file are in percent and are divided by 100. Density samples with a density correction larger than 0.10 g/cm³ are removed as bad hole, about 7% of the density interval. Density porosity is (2.65 − ρb) / (2.65 − 1.00), for a quartz matrix and water. In the limestone of the Houthem Formation a calcite matrix of 2.71 g/cm³ gives a density porosity a few porosity units higher. A shallow log from 0 to 462 m (gamma ray in counts per second, resistivity, SP) is not used.

CAL-GT-04 has no open-hole wireline logs in the available data. The logs used are gamma ray measured while drilling (LWD) in two runs, a Weatherford run from 815 m and a Scientific Drilling run from 1770 m to 3021 m, joined at 1771 m; drilling time from the mud-logging data; and temperature from a cased-hole log run in January 2016, shortly after drilling, so not at equilibrium temperature. Drilling time is the number of minutes to drill one meter. It changes with rock strength and porosity, and also with the bit, weight on bit and rotation speed, so it is a drilling parameter and not a rock property. The well is deviated and lies 0.5 to 1.4 km from the line, so its logs are compared with seismic traces up to 1.4 km from the rock that was logged. The other CAL-GT-04 log files on NLOG (multifinger caliper and electromagnetic casing inspection) describe the casing and are not used.

The ASTEN-GT-02 tops follow the palynological revision of TNO report 2021 R10829 (Munsterman, 2021). Compared with the 1987 tops, the Heksenberg Formation is removed, the Someren Member (707–857 m) and Wintelre Member (857–1173 m) of the Veldhoven Formation replace the lower Breda Formation and Veldhoven Clay, the Voort Member top moves to 1173 m, and the Dongen Formation is absent: the whole Eocene is missing at the Pyrenean unconformity at 1513 m, and the 1987 Basal Dongen Sand is the Reusel Member of the Landen Formation. The Houthem Formation top is at about 1635 m. The report depths are taken as measured below the same rotary table as the 1987 logs. The 1987 tops are kept in python/inputs/asten-02_tops_1987.csv.

3.2 Logs in two-way time

The logs are placed in two-way time along the well path with the same Dix interval velocities as the well tops, and averaged into 1 ms samples. Neither well has a sonic log or checkshot, so the tie between log and seismic can be several milliseconds off. At 4 ms sampling a shift of one or two samples changes which log values are paired with which attribute values.

3.3 Crossplots

At each 4 ms sample along the well path, the attribute value is read at the 20 m attribute trace nearest the projected path, and each log is averaged over a window centered on that sample, from 4 to 62 ms. A crossplot holds the samples inside the chosen interval where both values exist. The correlation coefficient is the Pearson correlation of those samples and is not computed for fewer than five samples. With the trend with time removed, a straight line against two-way time is fitted by least squares to each quantity over the samples shown and the differences from that line are plotted; the axes then span half the full range of each quantity, centered on zero.

Samples can also be grouped. An interval is the whole logged interval, one formation or member, or a group of adjacent units (the Someren to Boom members at ASTEN-GT-02, the Epen and Zeeland formations at CAL-GT-04). With a gamma ray cutoff, each sample is placed in a low or a high gamma ray class from the gamma ray averaged over the chosen window. For each attribute the separation of the two classes is the difference between the class means divided by their pooled standard deviation (Cohen's d), computed on the same samples as the crossplot and with the trend with time removed when that option is on. It is not computed when either class has fewer than five samples. Where one lithology lies above another, as the Epen shale above the Zeeland carbonate, the contrast between them is itself a change with two-way time, and removing a straight-line trend with time removes most of that contrast.

The tie shift moves the logs and formation tops of a well by a fixed two-way time, up to 16 ms either way, against the seismic before the logs are averaged at the attribute samples. On the bar charts each attribute is colored by what it measures: waveform attributes, which follow the oscillation of the trace or are band-limited (stack amplitudes, relative acoustic impedance, AVT, quadrature trace and phase), envelope and energy attributes, amplitude change with angle measured on envelopes, frequency attributes, geometric attributes and DQ attributes. The average of a waveform attribute over an interval is close to zero whatever the rock, so two classes rarely differ in its mean; quantitative interpretation reaches interval properties from these data by inversion with a low-frequency model. The tool contains no inversion, Vp/Vs, intercept or gradient, so it does not compare attributes with QI results; it shows how attributes can be tested against well logs before they are used alongside them. The guided step that sends attributes to the SOM ranks every attribute by the separation of the two gamma ray classes at the current well, interval and cutoff, with no tie shift or trend removal, and keeps the four largest, skipping any attribute that correlates at 0.9 or more along the well with one already kept.

When a DQ attribute is on the horizontal axis, the well panel shows DQ at its 2 ms sampling as an unfilled wiggle, averaged over the three 20 m traces nearest the projected well path, with the density porosity log drawn on the same track on a fixed scale from 0.15 to 0.45, increasing in the same direction as positive DQ, and a track of relative acoustic impedance. The correlation coefficient given above the DQ track pairs each 2 ms DQ sample with density porosity averaged over the same 2 ms, with the current tie shift. DQ changes sign from one half cycle to the next, so it is compared with the logs sample by sample and through the shape of the curves; its average over an interval is close to zero.

In part 1 of the exercise, samples at CAL-GT-04 are grouped by the well tops into the Houthem and Maastricht formations and the Landen Clay, Gelinden, Heers and Swalmen members above them, and the separation of the two groups is computed as for the gamma ray classes. CAL-GT-04 has no gamma ray above 815 m, so the tops take the place of a log there. The attribute along the Houthem pick is the mean of five 4 ms samples centered on the pick at each 20 m trace, with the pick interpolated linearly between its points. The Houthem classes of a SOM are the neurons of the samples between the Houthem top and base at CAL-GT-04, read along the projected well path; at the reveal, the same test is applied to the Houthem samples at ASTEN-GT-02.

Consecutive samples along the well are not independent. Most attributes are averaged over 62 ms, about 15 samples, so the number of independent samples is much smaller than the number of points, and a correlation from one well varies more from one interval to another than the number of points suggests.

In the two-attribute view the gray shading counts the samples of the study window (0–40 km, 0.15–2.0 s) in 64 × 64 bins of the two attributes, on a logarithmic scale. A closed polygon marks every sample along the whole line whose two attribute values fall inside it, tested on the stored 8-bit attribute values.

4. Self-organizing map

4.1 Input

For the chosen window (a range along the line and a range of two-way time), each chosen attribute is converted to a z-score using its mean and standard deviation over all samples in the window:

z = (a − mean) / standard deviation

Each sample is then a vector with one value per attribute. Because all attributes have unit variance, each has the same weight in the distance between samples; attributes that correlate strongly therefore count their shared information more than once.

4.2 Map and initialization

The map is a square grid of N = n × n neurons (n = 2 to 10) on a rectangular lattice. Each neuron holds a prototype vector with one value per attribute. The prototypes start on the plane of the first two principal components of the attributes, found by power iteration on the correlation matrix of 20,000 random samples from the window. A neuron in column u and row v (both scaled to −1…1) starts at

w = 2 ( u √λ₁ e₁ + v √λ₂ e₂ )

where λ₁, λ₂ are the two largest eigenvalues and e₁, e₂ their eigenvectors. The sign of each eigenvector is set so that its largest component is positive, which keeps the orientation of the map similar between runs.

4.3 Training

Training is sequential (online). At each step one of the 20,000 training samples x is drawn at random, its best-matching neuron b is found by Euclidean distance, and every neuron k within three neighborhood widths of b on the grid moves toward x:

wk ← wk + η(t) · exp( −g²bk / 2σ(t)² ) · ( x − wk )

where gbk is the distance between b and k on the grid. The neighborhood width σ decreases exponentially from half the grid width (at least 1) to 0.5, and the learning rate η from 0.5 to 0.02. The number of steps is 600 per neuron, with a minimum of 8,000. The random number generator uses a fixed seed, so the same attributes, window and grid size give the same map.

4.4 Classification and color

After training, every sample in the window is assigned to its nearest prototype. The color of a neuron depends only on its position on the grid: a bilinear blend of four corner colors (blue, red, yellow and green). Neighboring neurons hold similar prototypes and receive similar colors. The colors are not derived from the prototype values, and the neurons are not grouped into a smaller number of classes afterward.

4.5 Comparison with other SOM implementations

SOM implementations differ in several choices that change the result: sequential or batch training; a rectangular or hexagonal lattice; the neighborhood function and its decay; initialization from principal components or at random; whether prototypes are displayed on a fixed grid or projected into a continuous latent space; how colors are assigned (grid position, projected coordinates, or prototype values); and whether neurons are clustered into classes afterward. These are the settings to compare when a SOM from this page and one from another package look different.

5. SHAP values

5.1 What is explained

SHAP (Lundberg and Lee, 2017) is usually applied to a supervised model whose output is a number, such as a class probability. A SOM has no such output, so a quantity has to be chosen. Here the explained quantity is the sample's position on the neuron grid, because that position is what sets the sample's color on the section. A soft position is used, so that it changes smoothly as attribute values change:

p(x) = Σk mk(x) gk ,   mk(x) = exp( −(d²k − d²min) / τ ) / Σj exp( −(d²j − d²min) / τ )

where gk is the (column, row) of neuron k, dk the distance from the sample to prototype k, and τ the median squared distance from a training sample to its nearest prototype. The position has two components, so each attribute receives a SHAP value with two components; the plots show its length, divided by the grid width (n − 1) so that runs with different grid sizes can be compared.

For most samples the soft position lies close to the best-matching neuron. Where two neurons are nearly equally close, the soft position lies between them, while the section color comes from the single nearest neuron.

5.2 How the values are estimated

Shapley values average an attribute's contribution over every order in which attributes can be added. They are estimated by sampling orderings (Štrumbelj and Kononenko, 2014). For one ordering:

  1. A background sample is drawn at random from the training samples.
  2. Starting from the background sample's attribute values, the sample's own values are put in one attribute at a time, in a random order.
  3. The change in position caused by each attribute is recorded.

The SHAP value of an attribute is its average change over all orderings. The average position of the background samples plus the sum of all SHAP values equals the sample's position. This is an interventional estimate: attribute values are mixed between samples as if the attributes were independent.

5.4 Limitations

  • Correlated attributes share credit. Two attributes carrying the same information split its SHAP value, so each can look less important than the information they share. The list of attributes correlating at 0.8 or more in Build a SOM flags these.
  • Mixed samples. Putting one sample's value of an attribute next to another sample's values of the rest can create combinations that do not occur in the data.
  • Choice of output. A different explained quantity, such as the distance to the nearest prototype or the membership in one neuron, gives different SHAP values. Grid position was chosen because it is what the section colors show.
  • τ sets how quickly membership falls off with distance. A smaller τ makes the position jump between neurons; a larger τ pulls every position toward the center of the grid.
